## 1. Problem Statement

Develop an interactive learning application that turns a student's notes or study material into active learning. Students upload their material and use it to generate revision activities rather than simply reading content.

Suggested scope from the problem statement:

1. Document / text upload
2. Topic extraction and organization
3. Question-answer or flashcard generation
4. Practice quiz with scoring
5. Weak-topic and revision tracking

## 2. Understanding of the Problem

Students spend most of their revision time re-reading notes, which is passive and produces weak retention. Research on learning consistently shows that active recall (testing oneself) and spaced repetition (revisiting material at increasing intervals) are far more effective.

The gap is that creating good practice material from one's own notes is slow and manual. The problem is therefore not "chat with a document". It is:

- Convert unstructured notes into structured, topic-wise practice material automatically.
- Measure what the student actually knows, per topic.
- Direct the student back to what they are weak at, at the right time.

The system must decide what to ask, not wait to be asked. This is why retrieval alone is insufficient and why topic structure and learner tracking are central to the design.

## 3. Proposed Solution

NoteRecall is a web application that accepts a student's notes, organizes them into a topic hierarchy, generates grounded questions and flashcards for every topic, runs adaptive practice sessions, and maintains a per-topic mastery model that drives a spaced-repetition revision schedule.

Core principles:

- **Grounded:** every generated question is tied to a specific passage of the student's own notes and is verified against it.
- **Structured:** content is organized by topic and subtopic, not treated as a flat text pool.
- **Adaptive:** the next quiz is composed from what the student is weakest at and what is due for review.
- **Transparent:** the student can see why a question was asked, where the answer comes from, and how their mastery is calculated.

## 5. Target Users

- School and college students preparing for exams from their own class notes.
- Competitive exam aspirants working from large volumes of study material.
- Self-learners working from articles, textbooks, or course transcripts.

## 6. User Journey

1. The student creates a study space (for example, "Operating Systems") and uploads notes or pastes text.
2. The system processes the material and presents a topic outline. The student may rename, merge, split, or delete topics.
3. The system generates a question bank per topic in the background.
4. The student chooses a mode: flashcards, topic quiz, mixed quiz, or "Focus on weak areas".
5. After each session the student sees the score, per-topic breakdown, correct answers with explanations, and the source passage from the notes.
6. The dashboard updates mastery per topic and shows a "Revise next" list based on weakness and due dates.
7. On later days, the student returns to a daily revision queue generated by the spaced-repetition scheduler.

## 8. Detailed Pipeline

### 8.1 Ingestion

- **Inputs:** PDF, DOCX, TXT, Markdown, pasted text. Multiple files per study space.
- **Parsing:** text extraction with page and heading metadata preserved. Scanned PDFs handled through an optional OCR fallback.
- **Cleaning:** removal of headers, footers, page numbers, and repeated boilerplate; whitespace and encoding normalization.
- **Chunking:** structure-aware chunking that respects headings and paragraphs, targeting roughly 300 to 500 tokens per chunk with a small overlap. Each chunk stores document ID, page, heading path, and order.
- **Embedding:** each chunk is embedded and stored in the vector store with its metadata.

### 8.2 Topic Extraction and Organization

A hybrid approach is used so the result is both accurate and well-structured:

1. **Structure first:** if the document has headings or a table of contents, use them as the initial outline.
2. **Semantic clustering:** for unstructured notes, cluster chunk embeddings to find natural groupings.
3. **LLM naming and hierarchy:** the LLM names each cluster, groups clusters into topics and subtopics, and writes a one-line summary for each.
4. **Chunk tagging:** every chunk is assigned to exactly one subtopic (with an optional secondary topic).
5. **Student control:** the topic tree is editable. Renaming, merging, splitting, and deleting topics trigger a re-tag of affected chunks and regeneration where needed.

Each topic carries a coverage indicator (number of chunks, number of questions) so the student can see how well the notes are covered.

### 8.3 Question and Flashcard Generation

Generation runs per subtopic. For each subtopic, its chunks are retrieved (the RAG step) and passed to the LLM together with a strict output schema.

**Question types**

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Flashcard | Term/definition or question/answer pair for quick recall |
| MCQ | One correct answer and three plausible distractors, with explanation |
| Fill in the blank | A key term removed from a sentence taken from the notes |
| Short answer | Open response, graded by the LLM against the source |
| True / False with justification | For concept-check questions (optional) |

**Metadata stored with every question**

- Topic and subtopic
- Source chunk ID(s) and page reference
- Difficulty (easy, medium, hard)
- Cognitive level (recall, understanding, application)
- Correct answer, explanation, and (for MCQ) the distractors

**Structured output:** the LLM is required to return JSON conforming to a schema; malformed output is retried automatically.

### 8.4 Quality Control of Generated Content

Generated content is not trusted by default. Each item passes through a verification stage:

- **Answerability check:** a second LLM call confirms the answer is supported by the cited source chunk.
- **MCQ validity:** exactly one correct option; distractors are plausible but clearly wrong according to the source.
- **Duplicate removal:** near-duplicate questions are removed using embedding similarity.
- **Difficulty balance:** each subtopic is targeted for a mix of difficulty and cognitive levels.
- **Flagging:** students can flag a question as wrong or unclear; flagged questions are excluded from quizzes and queued for regeneration.

### 8.5 Practice Modes

| Mode | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Flashcards | Flip cards with self-rating (Again, Hard, Good, Easy) feeding the scheduler |
| Topic quiz | Questions from selected topics only |
| Mixed quiz | Balanced sample across all topics |
| Adaptive quiz ("Focus on weak areas") | Oversamples weak topics and due items |
| Daily revision | The queue of items due today according to the spaced-repetition schedule |

Quiz settings: number of questions, difficulty preference, question types, and an optional timer.

### 8.6 Scoring and Answer Evaluation

- **Objective questions (MCQ, fill in the blank, T/F):** exact or normalized matching.
- **Short answers:** the LLM grades the response against the reference answer and source passage, returning a score (0 to 1), a verdict (correct, partially correct, incorrect), missing points, and brief feedback.
- **Session result:** overall score, per-topic scores, time taken, and a review screen showing each question, the student's answer, the correct answer, the explanation, and the source passage.

### 8.7 Weak-Topic and Revision Tracking

**Mastery model.** For each topic, mastery is a value between 0 and 1 computed as a recency-weighted accuracy over the student's attempts, with adjustments for question difficulty and for partial credit on short answers. Recent attempts carry more weight than old ones, so mastery reflects current knowledge.

**Classification (thresholds are configurable):**

| Mastery | Label |
|---|---|
| Below 0.5 | Weak |
| 0.5 to 0.75 | Developing |
| Above 0.75 | Strong |

A topic needs a minimum number of attempts before it is classified, to avoid labelling on insufficient data.

**Adaptive quiz composition (default):** roughly 50% of questions from weak topics, 30% from developing topics or items due for review, and 20% from strong topics to prevent forgetting.

**Spaced repetition.** Each question or flashcard maintains a review state (ease factor, interval, repetitions, next due date) updated after every attempt using the SM-2 algorithm. Answer quality is mapped to the SM-2 quality scale from correctness, partial credit, response time, and the student's self-rating on flashcards. A simpler Leitner-box mode is available as a fallback.

**Revision support.** For a weak topic, the student can open a concise recap generated from the source chunks, then immediately retake a short targeted quiz on it.

### 8.8 Dashboard

- Mastery heatmap across the topic tree
- Weak, developing, and strong topic lists
- "Revise next" recommendations with reasons (for example, "Low accuracy, last practiced 6 days ago")
- Items due today and upcoming schedule
- Score trend over time and per-topic progress
- Study streak, questions attempted, and accuracy
- Attempt history with links back to the source passages

## 13. Key Differentiators

- Closed learning loop: generate, practice, measure, and revise, rather than upload and chat.
- Every question is grounded in and linked to the student's own notes, with a verification pass to limit hallucination.
- Adaptive quizzes and spaced repetition driven by a per-topic mastery model.
- Editable topic structure that keeps the student in control of how material is organized.
- Explainable recommendations: the system states why a topic is being suggested.
- Provider-agnostic LLM layer supporting privacy-preserving local deployment.

## 14. Privacy and Data Handling

- Notes and performance data are stored per user and are not shared across accounts.
- Local-model deployment allows all processing to remain on the student's or institution's own machine.
- Users can delete a study space, which removes its documents, chunks, embeddings, questions, and tracking data.

## 16. Assumptions and Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| LLM generates incorrect or ungrounded questions | Source-grounded prompts, verification pass, student flagging |
| Poor topic extraction on messy notes | Hybrid structure and clustering approach, editable topic tree |
| Poor text extraction from scanned or handwritten notes | OCR fallback; state limitations clearly for handwriting |
| Long processing times for large documents | Asynchronous jobs, incremental generation, progress display |
| Too little data to judge weak topics early | Minimum attempts threshold before classification |
| Unreliable short-answer grading | Grade against source with a rubric; show feedback and allow the student to dispute |
